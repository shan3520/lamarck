# First full run: Groq free tier (2026-10-02)

**Setup**
- Suite: `examples/acme-support/lamarck.groq.json`, with 15 train and 7 held-out tasks.
- Target (frozen): `openai/gpt-oss-20b`.
- Proposer: `openai/gpt-oss-120b`.
- Search: 4 iterations, k = 2 (screen), 3 (confirm), 3 (held-out).
- Cost: 417 rollouts, 177k tokens, about 45 minutes, **$0**.

## Result: SHIP
| | Seed | Evolved |
|---|---|---|
| Train score | 36.7% | 91.9% |
| **Held-out score (never seen)** | 39.7% | **96.0%** |
| **Held-out full-pass rate** | 0.0% | **85.7%** |
| Harness size | 93 chars | 896 chars |

- The in-loop gain was +55.2 pts and the held-out gain +56.3 pts, so the loop did **not** overfit its own tasks.
- The held-out gain is far above the noise band of 2.5 pts.
- The verdict rests on 21 held-out rollouts, above the 20-rollout minimum.

## What the loop learned on its own
It started from one sentence. In 4 iterations it discovered the hidden house conventions and wrote them as general rules:
- the sign-off
- the `Ticket #12345` format
- `USD 0.00` amounts
- the allowed priority codes
- the allowed category list
- a scoping rule

## Behaviors worth noting
- **The gates worked.** Two plausible-sounding edits were rejected because they lowered the score. One edit passed screening but failed the stricter re-test, so it was never trusted.
- **It fixed its own interference.** In iteration 4 the proposer noticed that its iteration-1 signature rule was leaking into the JSON triage tasks. It added a scoping rule ("no signature after JSON") and recovered those tasks. This is the cross-task interference DarwinX describes, repaired by the loop itself.

## Caveats (read before citing this)
1. **This is a toy benchmark I wrote.** Its verifiers' failure messages name the violated rule (e.g. "replies must be signed 'The Acme Support Team'"), so the loop can *read* the conventions off the failures. Real test suites often explain failures less clearly, so this is easier than real repos.
2. **Held-out tasks share the train tasks' conventions.** This shows generalization within one distribution, not to a different one.
3. **One of 5 conventions was never learned** ("don't promise timelines"). The model probably rarely violated it, so there was no signal.
4. **The harness has some redundancy**: the USD rule appears twice. An RRSI-style pruner would remove it, and it's on the to-do list.

## What this does and doesn't show
- **Shows:** the full engine works end to end on real models, at zero cost, and its safeguards behave as designed.
- **Doesn't show:** that it works on real teams' repositories. That's the next test: `lamarck mine-git` plus a coding agent on a real repo.
